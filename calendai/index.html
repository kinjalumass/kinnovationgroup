CalendAI

A planning engine disguised as a calendar

A calendar remembers things. It does not help you decide. CalendAI works out what to do next, when to do it, and what to give up when three things land on the same hour, then repairs the whole plan the moment a task runs long or a deadline moves.

The problem

The real schedule only exists in your head

Canvas deadlines sit in one place, Google Calendar in another, club meetings and career fairs nowhere at all, and food, sleep, the gym and calling home are held together by memory. It works until about week seven, and then it does not.

  • Planners assume nothing changes. Office hours move, assignments get rewritten, meetings overrun, and when the plan breaks most people abandon it rather than repair it.
  • The real cost is deciding. Which assignment first, office hours or the career fair, when to eat, how to climb back after a bad week. That is where the hours actually go.
  • Almost everyone underestimates how long work takes and no tool learns from it, so the next plan is just as unrealistic and you trust it a little less.
  • Getting five people in a room is painful. Shared calendars show you the gaps. They never tell you which gap is the good one.

Time is not simply free or busy. It has quality. A plan should bend instead of shattering, and the system should get sharper every time it watches you finish something.

The loop

Archive in, calendar out

  1. 01

    Connect

  2. 02

    Prioritize

    Tasks ranked by deadline, effort estimate and what they are worth to your grade.

  3. 03

    Block

  4. 04

    Repair

    Every completion or overrun re-optimizes whatever is left.

  5. 05

    Learn

    Duration estimates and your best hours sharpen with every cycle.

Inside

What is actually doing the work

1

Canvas turns into a calendar

Classes, sections, exams, office hours and deadlines import on their own, and grade weights come with them, so a quiz worth two percent stops outranking a project worth thirty.

2

Rescheduling from what you finished

Mark something done or half done and everything after it moves. Ran forty minutes over and the evening reshuffles. Finished early and the time goes to whatever was next in line.

3

Help with the tradeoff

When office hours, a team meeting and a career fair collide, it tells you which one to take and why, weighing grade impact, what the opportunity is worth, and the priorities you set yourself.

4

Work placed where you are good

It notices that maths goes faster before noon and that an evening gym session improves your sleep, then puts the hard blocks where you actually perform.

5

Scheduling for a group

Bring in several calendars with everyone consenting, and get real best fit options weighted for travel time, buffers and when people prefer to meet, instead of the first empty box.

Executive function as a piece of software. Students first, because their weeks are the hardest to hold together, then anyone whose days refuse to sit still.
