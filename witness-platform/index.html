Witness

A neutral evidence vault, not a reputational database

Thirty people see it happen. None of them know each other, none of them can be reached afterwards, and within a few hours the details have already blurred. Witness holds what people saw while it is still accurate, and lets the person it happened to find it, with consent required from everyone involved.

The gap

The evidence existed and there is no way to get it back

Harassment, discrimination, an accident, a threat, a confrontation that turns. People are there, and plenty of them would help. They are simply unreachable. Camera footage is overwritten inside a day or three. What is left is a justice gap built entirely out of things that were seen and never written down.

  • There is no safe way to write down what you saw without immediately becoming publicly involved in it.
  • Victims know other people watched it happen, and know the account is confirmable, and have no way at all to find them.
  • Eyewitness accuracy drops off within hours, and the small details that matter legally are the first ones to go.
  • Fear and embarrassment stop people recording their own account while it is fresh, which is the only window where it is worth much.

Record the event, never the person. Every hard problem in this space comes from systems that blur those two things together.

The sequence

Five steps, and a door that only opens outward

  1. 01

    Observe

    Somebody sees something they know is wrong.

  2. 02

    Record

    They log the event on their own. What, when, where. Not who.

  3. 03

    Seal

    Nothing is published. No feed, no profile, no exposure of any kind.

  4. 04

    Search

    Someone with standing searches the circumstances of their own incident.

  5. 05

    Consent

    The witness decides. Staying silent remains a real answer.

Commitments

Constraints, not features

Everything difficult about this problem comes from systems that collect more than they need. These are the limits the platform is built inside. They are not settings that get relaxed once there is a growth target.

  • Events, never people

    The record describes what happened. It never stores who someone is.

  • Nothing is published

    There is no feed, no profile, no public record. Sealed is the default state.

  • Contact requires consent

    A witness is asked, never surfaced. Declining leaves no trace and costs nothing.

  • No reputational scoring

    The platform builds no dossiers and ranks no one. That is the line it will not cross.

What it is

The mechanism

Logging an event

Timestamp, rough GPS location, what kind of incident it was, a neutral description of what happened, how many people were around, whether there were cameras. Media too, where the law allows it.

Searching by circumstance

A victim or their attorney searches a date, a time, a radius and an incident type. Never a person. The system has no concept of who anyone is.

Consent at the gate

If a log matches, the platform asks the witness. They decide whether to reply, how much to say, whether to stay anonymous, and whether to take part at all. No is a complete answer.

Help shaping the account

Secure chat or a call, tools that turn a recollection into something legally usable, encrypted export for authorised legal use, and payment for the witness time if they want it.

Built inside the legal limits

No names of accused people, no allegations attached to anyone identifiable, no profiles, no ratings, no background checks, and no AI put anywhere near identifying a face.

This is not surveillance. It is collective memory, kept carefully, and handed over only when the person holding it decides to.
